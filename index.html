About the track

Helping you navigate the huge amount of content at NeurIPS.

New to NeurIPS, new to AI, or just want help navigating the huge amount of content presented each year? The Education Track is a full-day primer on the biggest ideas in AI from the past year.

The day is built around concepts: any technical idea that has underpinned a large set of papers at top-tier AI conferences over the past year - from new architectures to training and alignment techniques to evaluation and reasoning methods. Think the 2026 equivalents of the Transformer, the reparameterisation trick, or LLM judges.

Each concept will be taught by leading science communicators and educators in AI, and paired with original educational resources - interactive demos, code notebooks and short videos to help you understand it. These will be added to an open repository, freely available to the community after the conference. There will also be poster sessions for deeper dives on the concepts.

Whether it's your first NeurIPS or your tenth, the track is designed to help you arrive prepared.

For submitters

Calling all science communicators in AI. The call invited proposals to teach an emerging concept shaping the field right now. Concepts can be any technical idea that has underpinned a large set of papers at top-tier AI conferences over the past year - from new architectures to training and alignment techniques to evaluation methods. Think the 2026 equivalents of the Transformer, the reparameterisation trick, or LLM judges. You need not be the original author of the concept - only that you can teach it with real clarity.

You'll need to pair your concept proposal with original teaching materials such as code notebooks or short videos. If accepted, you'll present in Sydney, and your materials will join the open repository for the community.

  • Teach one emerging AI concept with clarity
  • Include original notebooks, demos or short videos

Keynote speakers

Meet our keynote speakers.

Rachel Thomas

Answer.AI · Co-founder of fast.ai

Rachel Thomas was co-founder of fast.ai, which created the Practical Deep Learning for Coders course. She now works on R&D at Answer.AI. Previously, she was the founding director of the USF Center for Applied Data Ethics.

Rachel earned her mathematics PhD at Duke, was selected by Forbes as one of 20 Incredible Women in AI, and was an early engineer at Uber. You can find her writing at fast.ai and rachel.fast.ai.

Ben Leong

National University of Singapore

Dr Ben Leong is an Associate Professor of Computer Science at the National University of Singapore (NUS), with research interests in networking, distributed systems, and the application of AI in education.

He has received several teaching awards, including the NUS Outstanding Educator Award in 2015, and previously chaired the Computer Science Department Standing Teaching Committee. He has held various leadership roles, including Director of the Experimental Systems and Technology Laboratory at the Ministry of Education and Director of the Centre for Computing for Social Good & Philanthropy at NUS.

From 2020 to 2025, he served as Chief Data Officer of AI Singapore, the national AI programme. He is Director of the AI Centre for Educational Technologies (AICET), where his team develops AI-powered software platforms for education.
